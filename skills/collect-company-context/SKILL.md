# Collect company context

Help the user choose the target profiles most likely to advance the engagement’s goal. Save those choices and the company context that agents need to identify opportunities and prepare outreach.

1. Read [company-context-template.md](company-context-template.md) for the reusable document structure. Read the available company materials and research material gaps before interviewing. Establish the company’s current strategy and the engagement’s stated goal; present a provisional understanding rather than asking the user to repeat known information.

2. Ask one focused question at a time, only when the answer materially changes targeting, fit, outreach or success measurement. Include a short company-relevant example when it makes the question easier to answer, without limiting the possible answers. For example: “Who looks like a fit but usually isn’t—for example, the right industry but too small to need your offer?” Adapt follow-ups to the user’s answers.

3. Make target-profile selection the main discussion. Propose several distinct options, offering a real choice beyond the three final slots. For each, briefly describe who they are, the situation that makes them relevant, why they could advance the goal, and the supporting evidence or assumptions. Include direct audiences and connectors; a connector option should explain whom they could reach. Ask the user to pick the strongest options, combine or reject others, and explain what makes the winners promising. Refine the options together instead of choosing on the user’s behalf.

4. Confirm three selected target profiles: two ICP tracks and one connector track. ICP tracks may represent customers, investors or another audience relevant to the goal. Keep the company’s stable customer ICP separate from these engagement-specific choices. If the user has not chosen all three, retain the confirmed choices and surface the remaining decision; do not invent agreement to fill a slot. Approval means worth pursuing, not proven fit. Concrete matching hypotheses, real-person suggestions and feedback iterations belong to the separate opportunity routine.

5. Fill the reference using the companion template. Distinguish available capabilities from plans, actual results from targets, and facts from assumptions. Date baselines and time-sensitive claims; flag unresolved source conflicts. Aim for 600–800 words containing only information that changes targeting, fit, approach or success measurement. Do not pad missing information.

6. Save the draft in the agreed destination and have the user review the actual document, especially the goal, stable ICP and selected profiles. Incorporate corrections and obtain explicit validation; profile selection alone does not validate the whole document. Set **Last reviewed** on validation. Read back the saved content and summarize any remaining decisions. Revisions awaiting validation remain clearly marked as drafts.

For the current Notion workflow, use **Knowledge → Company context**, preserve its layout and section toggles, and store supporting materials in **Library** with evidence links beside the relevant claims. The companion template defines the information to collect independently of where the completed reference is stored.

This is the current company reference, not a learning log. Keep customer-specific information outside this skill.
